If you own a pool in Cyprus, you've probably noticed that keeping it clean isn't a fixed routine. What works in January falls apart by July. The island's climate swings hard between seasons, and your maintenance schedule needs to swing with it.

## Why Cyprus Makes Pool Maintenance More Demanding Than You'd Expect

Before getting into frequencies, it helps to understand what you're actually dealing with here. Cyprus sits in the eastern Mediterranean, which means intense UV radiation for most of the year, summer air temperatures regularly above 35°C, and some of the hardest tap water in Europe. Limassol and Paphos water sources pull from limestone-heavy geology, which drives calcium hardness levels up and creates scaling problems that pool owners in northern Europe simply don't face at the same severity.

Add to that the seasonal dust storms blowing in from the Sahara and the Middle East, heavy pollen in spring, and the sheer volume of bather activity that holiday rentals attract during peak season, and you have a set of conditions that genuinely stress a pool's water chemistry and filtration system.

## Summer Cleaning Frequency: June to September

During a Cyprus summer, a private residential pool should be cleaned and chemically checked at least twice a week. For holiday rentals or pools with high bather loads, three times a week is more appropriate.

This isn't overcaution. Here's why the frequency matters:

**Chlorine burns off fast.** At 38°C water temperature under direct Mediterranean sun, free chlorine dissipates far more quickly than the product label assumes. Those assumptions are typically based on temperate climates. Without stabiliser (cyanuric acid) at the right level and without regular dosing, your pool can go from balanced to unsafe in 48 hours.

**Algae establishes quickly in heat.** Green water doesn't take a week to develop in August. It can turn visibly green in three to four days if chlorine drops below the effective threshold. Catching that early with a mid-week visit is far cheaper than the chemical shock and extended filtration required to clear a full algae bloom.

**Calcium scaling accelerates.** High water temperatures combined with high calcium hardness and elevated pH create the conditions for calcium carbonate to deposit on tile grout, on fittings, and inside the filtration system. Regular brushing and pH management slow this significantly.

### What a Summer Service Visit Should Include

Each visit should cover: skimming and vacuuming, brushing the walls and steps, backwashing or rinsing the filter if pressure has risen, testing and adjusting pH, chlorine, alkalinity, and stabiliser, and a visual check of pump basket and skimmer basket. Testing calcium hardness and TDS (total dissolved solids) should happen at least once a month.

## Winter Cleaning Frequency: October to March

Cyprus winters are mild by European standards, but they're not irrelevant for pool owners. Water temperatures drop to around 14–18°C depending on whether the pool is heated, and bather use drops sharply. For most private pools, one visit per week is sufficient. For unheated pools that aren't being used at all, you can in some cases manage with visits every ten to fourteen days, provided you're monitoring remotely or checking in between.

The temptation is to assume that because the pool isn't being used much, it's looking after itself. It isn't. Rainfall, especially in the wetter winter months from November through February, dilutes and disrupts your water chemistry. Dust and organic debris still fall into the pool. Algae grows more slowly in cooler water, but it still grows, particularly on shaded walls or steps.

### The Specific Risks in Winter

**Dust storms don't stop in winter.** Cyprus sees Saharan dust events year-round, and a storm can deposit a visible film on pool water within hours. This affects both the appearance and the water chemistry, particularly pH, which tends to rise after significant dust contamination.

**Unbalanced water causes damage.** A pool sitting with low chlorine and drifting pH through three months of winter isn't just a cosmetic problem. Aggressive water, with low calcium hardness and low pH, etches plaster and attacks grout. High pH and calcium deposit scale on heating elements if the pool is heated. Neither is cheap to fix.

## Should You Reduce Service to Once a Fortnight?

This question comes up regularly, especially from owners trying to cut costs over winter. The honest answer depends on your specific pool. Pools with good covers, no trees nearby, no guests, and a stable existing chemistry can sometimes stretch to ten to fourteen days in the coldest months. Pools exposed to wind, under carob or eucalyptus trees, or with older plaster surfaces that are harder to keep balanced should stay on a weekly schedule.

Trying to save on monthly service costs by skipping visits often results in spending more on chemicals, shock treatments, and occasionally on tile cleaning or filter maintenance that proper routine would have prevented.

## A Practical Year-Round Framework

To summarise it simply: two visits per week from June through September, one visit per week from October through May, with adjustments up or down depending on bather load, tree coverage, weather events, and whether the pool is heated. Every pool is slightly different, and any decent maintenance company should be adjusting your schedule and dosing based on what's actually happening in your water, not running on autopilot.
