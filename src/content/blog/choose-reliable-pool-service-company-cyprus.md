Finding a reliable pool service company in Cyprus is harder than it should be. The island has no shortage of people offering pool cleaning, but the quality gap between a professional operation and someone with a pump and a brush is enormous. If you own a villa, manage holiday rentals, or simply want your pool to stay safe and clear through a long Cyprus summer, here is what to actually look for.

## Check Their Understanding of Local Water Conditions

Cyprus has some of the hardest tap water in Europe, with calcium hardness levels in many areas regularly exceeding 400 ppm. This is not a minor detail. A company that does not actively manage calcium hardness and total dissolved solids will leave you with scaling on your tile line, cloudy water, and damaged equipment, often within a single season.

Ask directly: how do they handle calcium scaling? What is their approach to stabiliser (cyanuric acid) levels during summer, when high UV radiation burns through free chlorine faster than in northern climates? Do they adjust chemical dosing after a dust storm, which deposits fine particulate into the water and throws off pH and turbidity?

If you get a vague answer, that is a red flag. A competent technician should be able to explain, in plain terms, how they balance water chemistry for Cypriot conditions, not just add chlorine and leave.

### What a Professional Water Test Should Include

A basic dip strip is not a professional water test. Look for a company that measures at minimum: free chlorine, combined chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, and stabiliser. Some will also test for metals, which matters if you are on a property that draws from a borehole or an older supply.

## Verify Their Equipment and Maintenance Practices

A good pool service company arrives with proper equipment, not improvised substitutes. For routine cleaning this means a commercial-grade vacuum or automatic cleaning head, telescopic poles with quality brushes suited to your surface type (important if you have fibreglass rather than plaster), a leaf net, and a water testing kit. For filter maintenance, they should know the difference between servicing a sand filter, a cartridge filter, and a DE filter, and treat each accordingly.

Ask who services the pump and filter when there is a problem. Some companies clean but will not touch equipment, leaving you to find a separate technician. Others handle the full mechanical side. Know what you are buying before you sign up.

### Equipment-Related Questions Worth Asking

- Do they carry spare parts for common pump models used in Cyprus?
- How do they handle a high-pressure reading on the filter gauge?
- Do they backwash on a fixed schedule, or based on pressure differential?

The last question is a simple way to distinguish someone who understands filtration from someone following a checklist without knowing why.

## Look for Consistency, Not Just a Low Price

Price is obviously a factor. But in pool maintenance, the cheapest option usually means one of three things: irregular visits, undertrained staff, or corners being cut on chemicals. In Cyprus, where pools often run from March through November and sometimes year-round, inconsistent service leads to algae blooms, chloramine buildup, and the kind of water quality problems that cost far more to fix than to prevent.

Ask for a clear schedule. How often will they visit? Is it the same technician each time, or a rotating crew? Will they send a report or photo after each visit so you can see what was done? For holiday rental managers especially, documentation matters. If a guest complains about water quality, you want a service record to refer back to.

A reliable company should also communicate proactively. If they notice your pump is making unusual noise, or that your salt cell needs cleaning, they should tell you without waiting to be asked.

## Understand What the Contract Actually Covers

Service agreements in Cyprus vary widely in what they include. Some cover chemicals, some do not. Some include equipment repairs up to a certain value, others exclude all mechanical work. Before committing, get clarity on:

- Whether chemicals are included or billed separately
- Whether filter media replacement (sand, cartridge) is covered
- What happens if there is a water quality problem between scheduled visits
- Whether they offer any callout service for urgent issues

For villa and holiday rental owners, it is worth asking specifically how they handle service during peak summer season, when demand on local companies is highest. A company that overbooks in July and August will not give your pool the attention it needs precisely when it matters most.

## Ask for References and Check Their Presence

Word of mouth still carries significant weight in Cyprus, particularly in expat and villa owner communities around Limassol, Paphos, and Larnaca. Ask the company for references from existing clients, ideally in your area and with a similar pool type. A business that has been operating consistently for several years and can point to satisfied clients in your region is a reasonable starting point.

Check whether they have a professional website, a registered business, and some form of online presence. This is a basic indicator that they are operating as a legitimate company rather than an informal arrangement.
