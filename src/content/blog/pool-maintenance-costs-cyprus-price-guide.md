If you own a pool in Cyprus, you already know it's not a seasonal luxury — it's a year-round responsibility. The climate that makes Cyprus ideal for swimming also makes it demanding on pool systems: intense UV, extreme summer heat, hard water, airborne dust, and the occasional Saharan dust storm all affect how quickly a pool degrades without proper care. Understanding what maintenance actually costs, and why, helps you budget realistically and avoid the more expensive mistakes.

## What Drives Pool Maintenance Costs in Cyprus

Cyprus has some of the hardest tap water in Europe. The island's limestone geology means water drawn from boreholes and municipal supplies carries high levels of calcium and magnesium. This mineral load accelerates scaling on pool surfaces, pipework, and heat pump components. It also makes water balance harder to maintain — pH can drift quickly, and total alkalinity fluctuates more than it would in a softer-water country. You'll use more chemicals here than a comparable pool in northern Europe would require, and your equipment will need closer attention.

UV intensity is the other major factor. Chlorine degrades faster under strong sunlight, so stabiliser levels matter more, and dosing needs to be more precise. Pools in uncovered or south-facing positions can lose effective chlorine within hours on a July afternoon. This isn't a reason to panic — it's just a reason to know what you're working with.

## Typical Monthly Maintenance Costs

Costs vary depending on pool size, location, and the level of service you need. The figures below reflect what most residential pool owners in Limassol, Paphos, Nicosia, and Larnaca are actually paying.

### Regular Weekly Servicing

A standard weekly visit from a professional pool technician — covering water testing, chemical adjustment, brushing, vacuuming, and filter checks — typically costs between €80 and €150 per month for a residential pool of 30 to 60 cubic metres. Larger pools, pools with water features, or those attached to holiday rentals with heavy bather loads will sit toward the higher end or beyond it.

### Chemicals

Expect to spend €30 to €80 per month on chemicals alone, depending on pool size and season. Summer months cost more. Pools that sit in full sun through August will consume more chlorine, pH corrector, and stabiliser than the same pool in October. Anti-scale treatments are not optional in Cyprus — they're a regular maintenance cost, not an occasional one.

### Filter and Equipment Maintenance

Sand filter media should be replaced every three to five years. A full sand change on a standard residential filter costs €80 to €200 depending on the size of the tank and the grade of sand used. Cartridge filters need cleaning every few weeks in high season and replacement annually or biannually. DE filters are less common on the island but follow similar logic.

Salt chlorination systems, which are popular in Cyprus, require cell inspection and cleaning every few months. Calcium deposits build up on the electrolytic plates, particularly given the hard water, and a neglected cell will fail prematurely. Replacement cells cost €150 to €400 depending on the unit.

## Annual and One-Off Costs to Budget For

Beyond monthly servicing, there are predictable annual expenses that catch owners off guard if they haven't planned for them.

**Acid washing or deep cleaning.** Pools that haven't been maintained through winter, or that have accumulated algae staining or heavy scale, will need an acid wash at some point. This runs from €150 to €400 depending on pool condition and size. It's not something you need every year with good maintenance, but skipping winter care often makes it necessary.

**Heat pump or heater servicing.** If you run your pool through winter or shoulder seasons — and many villa owners and rental operators in Cyprus do — the heat pump needs an annual service check. Scale buildup on the evaporator and condenser affects efficiency significantly. Budget €80 to €150 for a professional service.

**Replastering or resurfacing.** Pool plaster has a lifespan of roughly 10 to 15 years under normal conditions, though hard water and high UV can shorten this. A full replaster on a standard pool in Cyprus costs €3,000 to €7,000 or more, depending on surface material and pool geometry. This isn't a frequent cost, but it should sit somewhere in your long-term planning.

## Holiday Rental Pools: A Different Calculation

If you're managing a villa or holiday rental, the cost-benefit calculation shifts. Your guests will use the pool heavily, and a cloudy or green pool directly affects reviews and rebooking rates. Most rental operators on the island choose twice-weekly servicing through the high season, which adds to the monthly spend but reduces the risk of a problem developing between visits. Some also install automated dosing systems — the upfront cost is €500 to €1,500, but they reduce chemical waste and keep conditions more consistent between technician visits.

## Getting the Numbers Right for Your Pool

The honest answer is that a well-maintained pool in Cyprus costs more to run than many owners expect when they first install one. A realistic annual budget for a mid-sized residential pool, including professional servicing, chemicals, and minor consumables, sits somewhere between €1,500 and €3,000. That figure rises if your pool has additional features, if you heat it year-round, or if it's in a high-use rental context.

The cheapest outcome is consistent, preventive care. Pools that are neglected and then fixed reactively almost always end up costing more than pools that are maintained properly from the start.
