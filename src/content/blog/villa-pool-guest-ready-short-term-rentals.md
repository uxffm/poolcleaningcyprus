If you're renting out your villa in Cyprus, the pool is often the main reason guests booked. A cloudy pool, a broken light, or a strong chemical smell on arrival day can tank your reviews before anyone has even unpacked. Getting the pool genuinely ready, not just visually tidy, takes a bit of system and some Cyprus-specific knowledge.

## Chemistry First — Don't Guess, Test

The Mediterranean climate means your pool chemistry shifts faster than it would in northern Europe. High temperatures accelerate chlorine loss, bathers introduce organic load quickly, and Cyprus has some of the hardest water in Europe, with calcium hardness levels that can cause scaling on surfaces and equipment if left unchecked.

Before any guest arrival, test for:

- **Free chlorine** (target 1–3 ppm)
- **pH** (7.2–7.6 is the range to hold)
- **Total alkalinity** (80–120 ppm keeps pH stable)
- **Calcium hardness** (200–400 ppm — above this, you risk scale deposits on the tile line and inside the heat pump)
- **Cyanuric acid / stabiliser** (30–50 ppm; too high and chlorine loses effectiveness even if the reading looks fine)

Cyanuric acid is a common problem in rental pools. Stabiliser accumulates over time and doesn't evaporate or get used up. In an outdoor pool under the Cyprus sun, you need it, but if it creeps above 80–100 ppm, your chlorine becomes far less effective. The fix is a partial water change. There's no chemical shortcut.

## Equipment Check — What Guests Will Notice and What They Won't

Guests don't look at your filter pressure gauge. They do notice a pump that sounds like a grinder, a skimmer basket full of debris on day one, or a pool light that flickers or doesn't work. A pre-arrival equipment check should cover:

### Filtration and circulation
Run the system and check that the pump is priming properly and that filter pressure is within normal operating range for your setup. If your sand filter hasn't been backwashed in several weeks, do it. If you're on a cartridge filter, pull the cartridge and inspect it. A clogged filter in August can turn a clear pool murky within 48 hours of heavy use.

### Salt chlorinator cell (if applicable)
Many villas in Cyprus run salt systems. The cell plates are prone to calcium scaling because of the hard water, and a scaled cell produces less chlorine than it should. Check the cell and clean it with a dilute acid solution if there's visible white deposit. If the salt reading on the controller is erratic or the cell inspection lamp is on, investigate before the guests arrive, not during their stay.

### Lighting and pool surrounds
Replace any failed underwater lights. Check that poolside lighting is working. Look at the coping tiles and pool surround for anything that might be a trip hazard — cracked tile, a raised edge — and document it.

## Between Stays: Cleaning That Actually Matters

Brushing the walls and floor once before guest arrival is fine for a pool that's been maintained regularly. If the previous guests were there for two or three weeks in July and the pool had heavy use, you need more than a skim and a top-up of chlorine.

Brush the entire pool, including the waterline. Biofilm and oils accumulate at the surface — a waterline ring is partly cosmetic but also a hygiene issue. Vacuum to waste if the pool has visible debris on the floor rather than recycling dirty water back through the filter.

Dust storms from North Africa are a seasonal reality in Cyprus. They can drop a significant load of fine reddish-brown dust into a pool within hours. If guests arrive shortly after one, a standard clean isn't enough. You may need to floc the pool, let the particles settle, and vacuum to waste to clear it properly.

## Water Level, Heating, and Presentation

Check the water level. Evaporation in summer, combined with splash-out from use, can drop the level enough that the skimmer starts drawing air, which can damage the pump. Fill to mid-skimmer before guests arrive.

If the pool has a heat pump, agree with the guest or rental manager on a target temperature and get the system running early enough that the pool is actually at that temperature on arrival day. Heating from 26°C to 28°C in a large pool takes time. In spring or autumn, budget for at least 24 hours.

Clear the pump basket, check the chemical feeder or dispenser, and leave the pool looking like it's been properly looked after — because it has been.

## Maintenance During the Stay

For stays of more than four or five days, especially in peak summer, the pool chemistry will drift. A mid-stay service visit makes practical sense. One visit at the halfway point to test, adjust, and skim keeps the pool comfortable and protects your equipment and surfaces. It also gives you eyes on anything that might be going wrong — a pump noise, a developing algae patch — before it becomes a problem for the guest or a repair bill for you.

Some rental managers schedule every-other-day visits during peak season. That's not excessive. In July and August in Limassol or Paphos, with a full villa and eight-hour days of swimming, a pool can go from balanced to problematic faster than most people expect.
