If you own a pool in Cyprus, you already know that "clean it occasionally" isn't a maintenance strategy. The climate here is genuinely demanding, and what works in a mild northern European garden will fall apart fast under a Cypriot summer sun. The honest answer to how often you should clean your pool is: it depends on the season, and the difference between summer and winter is more significant than most people expect.

## Why Cyprus Is Harder on Pools Than Most Places

Before getting into schedules, it helps to understand what you're actually dealing with.

Cyprus has some of the highest UV index readings in the Mediterranean. UV light degrades chlorine rapidly, meaning chemical treatments that would last several days in cooler climates can be neutralised within hours during July and August. The island also sits in a hard water zone, with calcium carbonate levels in tap water that are among the highest in Europe. That calcium deposits on pool surfaces, waterline tiles, and filtration equipment steadily over time, and the problem accelerates in summer when evaporation is high.

Add to that the seasonal dust storms that blow in from North Africa, carrying fine particulate matter that settles across every outdoor surface. After a Saharan dust event, a pool that looked clean on Tuesday can have a visible haze by Wednesday morning. For holiday rental managers and villa owners who need pools guest-ready at short notice, this is a real operational problem.

## Summer Cleaning Frequency: Every Week at Minimum

From May through October, weekly professional cleaning is the baseline for a well-maintained pool. For pools with heavy bather loads — holiday rentals, villas hosting large groups — twice-weekly is often more appropriate.

### What Needs Doing Each Week

Skimming and brushing should happen at least twice a week during peak summer, even if a full service visit only happens once. The combination of sunscreen residue, body oils, high bather numbers, and airborne dust creates the conditions for algae blooms and cloudy water quickly. Phosphates from sunscreen are a particular problem because they act as a nutrient source for algae.

Water chemistry needs testing and adjusting weekly at minimum. In high summer, pH drift happens fast because of the heat, the evaporation rate, and the constant addition of fill water, which in Cyprus carries its own mineral load. Free chlorine levels should be checked more frequently — ideally two or three times per week — because UV degradation is so aggressive. If you're not using a stabiliser (cyanuric acid) in your outdoor pool during summer, you're likely wasting a significant portion of your chlorine spend.

Filter backwashing frequency should increase in summer. Sand filters that might need backwashing every two weeks in winter may need it weekly or more often when the pool is in heavy use.

### Evaporation and Top-Up Water

Cyprus pools can lose three to five centimetres of water per week to evaporation in July and August. Every time you top up with mains water, you're adding more calcium hardness and more minerals. Over a summer, this compounds significantly. A responsible maintenance routine tracks calcium hardness levels and addresses rising TDS (total dissolved solids) before they start scaling the heat exchanger in your heater or the walls of the pool itself.

## Winter Cleaning Frequency: Every Two to Three Weeks

From November through March, the demands change considerably. Bather load drops off, UV intensity decreases, water temperatures fall, and algae growth slows. But "less demanding" doesn't mean "no maintenance."

### What Still Needs Attention in Winter

Rainfall brings its own contamination. Winter storms deposit organic material, dirt, and debris, and can shift water chemistry significantly — particularly pH, which rain tends to lower. After heavy rainfall, it's worth checking chemistry even if your next scheduled service is a week away.

Leaf fall is a genuine issue in many gardens in Cyprus, particularly from November onwards. Decomposing leaves consume chlorine and provide a food source for algae. If your pool is near trees, more frequent skimming — even in winter — saves you from a harder cleanup later.

Dust storms don't stop in winter. If anything, the aftermath of a dust event in the rainy season can be worse because the rain drives the particles into the water rather than leaving them sitting on the surface.

The practical upshot: every two to three weeks is a reasonable winter service interval for most private pools in Cyprus, with chemistry checks and skimming in between as needed.

## Mistakes That Cost More Later

Skipping winter maintenance to save money is one of the most common decisions that leads to expensive remediation in spring. A pool that runs without proper chemical balance for several months can develop calcium scale on the shell, staining on the waterline, or a biofilm that requires an acid wash or aggressive shocking to clear before the season opens. The cost of consistent, modest maintenance through winter is almost always lower than the cost of getting a neglected pool back to standard in April.

Similarly, under-maintaining during summer — relying on a fortnightly service when weekly is genuinely needed — risks algae blooms that close a pool for days. For rental operators, that has a direct financial cost beyond just the remediation bill.

## A Simple Seasonal Schedule to Work From

- **May to October:** Professional service weekly. Chemistry checks two to three times per week. Filter backwash as needed, often weekly.
- **November to April:** Professional service every two to three weeks. Post-rain chemistry checks. Skimming after storms and dust events.

Adjust based on your pool's specifics — bather load, surrounding vegetation, whether it's heated, and how much direct sun it receives will all shift these numbers.
