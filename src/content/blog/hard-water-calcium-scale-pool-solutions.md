Cyprus has some of the hardest tap water in Europe. If you own a pool here, you've almost certainly seen it — white crusty deposits around the waterline, a rough chalky feel on the steps and walls, cloudy water that won't clear no matter how much chlorine you add. This isn't a maintenance failure. It's a predictable consequence of filling and topping up your pool with water drawn from limestone-rich aquifers. Understanding what's actually happening — and what to do about it — will save you money, protect your pool surfaces, and keep the water looking right.

## Why Cyprus Water Is So Hard

The geology of the island is largely limestone and chalk. As rainwater filters through the ground, it dissolves calcium and magnesium carbonates, which end up in the groundwater supply. By the time that water reaches your pool, it's carrying high levels of dissolved minerals — typically calcium hardness readings of 300–500 ppm or more straight from the tap, sometimes higher in areas like Limassol and Larnaca.

Add to that the intense evaporation Cyprus summers bring. When water evaporates from your pool, it takes nothing with it — not the calcium, not the magnesium. Those minerals stay in the water and concentrate. Over a long hot summer with heavy evaporation, you can watch your calcium hardness climb well above 600 or even 800 ppm. At those levels, the water becomes saturated and starts depositing calcium carbonate onto every surface it touches.

## What Calcium Scale Actually Does to Your Pool

Scale isn't just cosmetic. Left unaddressed, it causes real damage.

### Surface Damage

On plaster and rendered pools, scale bonds tightly to the surface and becomes increasingly difficult to remove. What starts as a thin white film becomes hard nodular deposits that can only be removed by acid washing or, in bad cases, replastering. On tiled pools, scale settles into grout lines and along the waterline tiles, eventually causing discolouration and cracking if water gets under deposits and expands.

### Equipment Problems

Calcium scale forms inside pipes, on heat exchanger surfaces, and on salt chlorinator cells. On a salt cell, scale reduces efficiency significantly — the cell has to work harder, runs hotter, and wears out faster. We regularly see cells that should last five or six years failing in two or three because of scale buildup that was never properly managed.

### Water Clarity

High calcium levels, especially combined with high pH (which is also common when using calcium hypochlorite tablets, as many Cyprus pool owners do), push water into a supersaturated state. The water clouds up — sometimes severely — and doesn't respond normally to chemical treatment. This is often misdiagnosed as an algae problem or a filtration issue, when the actual cause is calcium precipitation.

## How to Keep Calcium Under Control

### Know Your Numbers

Test your calcium hardness regularly — every two weeks in summer is not excessive. The target range is 200–400 ppm. Anything above 400 ppm puts you at increasing risk of scale; above 500 ppm and you need to act. A proper test kit or a visit from a professional gives you the full picture alongside pH, alkalinity, and your Langelier Saturation Index (LSI), which tells you whether your water is actively scaling, balanced, or corrosive.

### Use a Sequestering Agent

A sequestrant (sometimes called a scale inhibitor or metal/scale remover) binds to calcium and magnesium ions in the water and keeps them in solution rather than allowing them to deposit on surfaces. This is one of the most useful products in a Cyprus pool owner's toolkit. Dose it monthly as a preventative, or more frequently if your hardness is already high. It won't lower your calcium hardness reading — it just stops the calcium from causing damage.

### Partial Drain and Refill

The most effective long-term solution is dilution. If your calcium hardness is consistently above 500–600 ppm, the only way to bring it down is to remove some water and replace it with fresh. A partial drain of 20–30% of the pool volume, done in cooler months when evaporation is slower, can significantly reset your calcium levels. This needs to be done carefully — draining too much in dry conditions can crack a fibreglass pool shell or cause a plaster pool to pop out of the ground if the water table is high.

### Maintain pH Carefully

High pH dramatically accelerates scale formation. Aim to keep pH between 7.2 and 7.6. If you're using trichlor tablets (which lower pH over time), this may take care of itself. If you're using calcium hypochlorite or sodium hypochlorite, you'll likely need to add pH minus regularly. This is one area where consistent monitoring pays off more than any single chemical treatment.

### Acid Washing Existing Scale

If scale has already built up on your pool walls, steps, or waterline tiles, mechanical scrubbing combined with diluted muriatic acid can remove it. This is a job that requires care — acid splashes on skin or eyes are serious, and adding acid incorrectly to pool water can cause rapid pH swings that damage surfaces further. ## Dust Storms and Minerals From the Air

One thing many pool owners don't consider: Cyprus's periodic Saharan dust storms add mineral load directly to your pool. After a heavy dust event, your pool water chemistry can shift noticeably. Run your filter longer after these events and check your water balance — particularly pH and alkalinity — before assuming your normal routine is enough.
