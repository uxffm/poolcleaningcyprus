If you've ever watched a pool technician work through a service visit and wondered exactly what they're doing — and whether you're getting your money's worth — this post is for you. A professional pool cleaning service covers considerably more than skimming leaves and adding chlorine. Here's what should actually happen during a proper visit, and why each step matters in Cyprus conditions specifically.

## Water Testing and Chemical Adjustment

This is the foundation of every service visit, and it's where experience counts most.

A technician should test for free chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, cyanuric acid (stabiliser), and calcium hardness. In Cyprus, calcium hardness deserves particular attention. The island's water supply is drawn largely from limestone-heavy aquifers, which means tap water already arrives with elevated calcium levels. Fill a pool here repeatedly over a season and you're building toward scaling on tile lines, plaster, and equipment surfaces faster than you would in most European countries.

pH also drifts quickly in Cyprus conditions. High summer temperatures and intense UV exposure accelerate chlorine consumption and push pH upward. A pool sitting untreated for two weeks in August is a very different body of water from one maintained on a weekly schedule.

After testing, the technician adjusts chemicals as needed: adding chlorine (usually as liquid sodium hypochlorite or granular), correcting pH with acid or buffer, and topping up stabiliser if it has dropped below effective levels. Stabiliser matters here because Cyprus sunlight degrades unstabilised chlorine within hours. Without adequate cyanuric acid in the water, you're essentially adding chlorine and watching it disappear.

## Physical Cleaning: Surfaces and Waterline

Once the chemistry is set, the physical cleaning begins. This typically involves:

### Skimming and Debris Removal

Surface debris — leaves, insects, dust, pollen — is removed from the water. In spring and autumn, Saharan dust storms deposit a visible reddish-brown film across outdoor surfaces in Cyprus, including pool water. A good technician will not just skim the surface but check whether the dust has already settled to the floor and address it accordingly.

### Brushing

Pool walls and steps are brushed to break up algae and calcium deposits before they establish. Brushing is not optional. It dislodges biofilm and loose mineral deposits that vacuuming alone will miss. In pools with plaster or rendered finishes, which are common in older Limassol and Paphos villas, regular brushing is especially important to prevent calcium scale from bonding permanently to the surface.

### Vacuuming

The pool floor is vacuumed either manually or using an automatic cleaner checked and directed by the technician. Fine sediment, calcium particles, and settled dust are removed. If an automatic cleaner is part of the setup, the technician should inspect it for wear, check that it's covering the floor properly, and clear any blockages.

### Waterline Cleaning

The waterline tile or liner edge is where oils, sunscreen, and calcium scale accumulate most visibly. Regular light cleaning prevents this from hardening into a band of scale that requires acid treatment or mechanical removal later. This step often gets skipped in basic or budget services, which is worth asking about when you're comparing quotes.

## Equipment Inspection and Basket Cleaning

A service visit that only cleans the water and ignores the equipment is incomplete.

The technician should empty and rinse the skimmer basket and pump basket. A blocked basket restricts flow and puts strain on the pump motor — one of the more expensive components to replace. They should also check the pump for unusual noise, verify that the filter pressure is within normal operating range, and confirm that the backwash or rinse cycle has been run if pressure is elevated.

In Cyprus, sand filters are the most common type. Sand should be replaced every three to five years depending on usage and water quality; a good technician will flag this when it's due rather than waiting for you to notice the water staying cloudy. Cartridge filters need rinsing at each visit and periodic deep cleaning with filter cleaner solution.

Checking the heater connections, salt chlorinator cell (if present), and any automatic dosing equipment also falls within a thorough service. Salt cells in particular accumulate calcium deposits in Cyprus water and need descaling every few months to maintain output.

## What You Should Receive After Each Visit

A professional service should leave you with clear water, a verbal or written note of what was adjusted and why, and any observations about developing issues. If the technician noticed the pump making early bearing noise, or spotted a small crack developing near a return jet fitting, you should know about it before it becomes an emergency repair.

Some companies provide a basic chemical log, either on paper or digitally, so you can see the trend in your water parameters over time. This is particularly useful for holiday rental managers who aren't on-site every week and need documentation that the pool has been maintained to a standard.

## How Often Should a Pool in Cyprus Be Serviced?

For a residential pool in regular use, weekly service during summer (April through October) is the practical standard. Bi-weekly visits may be adequate in the cooler months if the pool is covered and not being used, but do not stretch this further, especially in Larnaca or coastal Limassol where windblown salt and dust make a difference.

If your pool is part of a holiday rental, weekly service is not negotiable — water quality affects reviews, and an unbalanced pool can irritate guests' eyes and skin within a single day.
