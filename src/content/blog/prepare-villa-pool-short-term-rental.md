If you rent out a villa in Cyprus, your pool isn't a luxury feature — it's the reason most guests book. A poorly maintained pool can generate bad reviews faster than anything else on your property. Getting it consistently right takes more than a weekly brush and a bag of chlorine. Here's what actually matters before guests arrive.

## Set a Baseline Before the Season Starts

Before your first booking of the year, carry out a full service rather than just topping up chemicals. This means cleaning the filter media (or replacing sand if it's past its useful life, typically five to seven years), inspecting the pump and skimmer baskets, checking the O-rings and pressure gauge on the filter, and testing a full chemical panel — not just free chlorine, but also pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, cyanuric acid, and TDS.

Cyprus has some of the hardest tap water in Europe, due to the island's limestone geology. Calcium hardness in Limassol and Paphos tap water regularly exceeds 400 mg/l, sometimes significantly. Filling or topping up your pool with this water without active management causes calcium scale to build up on tiles, fittings, and inside the filter over time. Check your calcium hardness at the start of the season and know what you're working with. If it's already high from the previous year, you may need a partial drain before guests arrive.

Cyanuric acid (CYA) is the other figure that rental pool owners often ignore. It acts as a stabiliser, protecting chlorine from being destroyed by UV. Cyprus gets around 330 days of sunshine per year, and a pool with no stabiliser will burn through chlorine extremely quickly. However, if CYA climbs above 80–100 mg/l, it begins to reduce chlorine's effectiveness even when chlorine readings look fine. If your pool has been running on stabilised chlorine tablets (trichlor) for several seasons without a partial drain, your CYA is probably too high. Test it.

## Build a Cleaning Schedule Around Guest Turnovers

The standard approach of a once-weekly visit may be adequate for a private pool used occasionally. For a holiday rental in peak season, it is not.

During July and August in Cyprus, pool water evaporates quickly, bather loads can be high, and the combination of sunscreen, sweat, and debris creates a significant demand on your sanitiser. If guests check in every week, the pool should be serviced either just before each new arrival or twice weekly during high season.

### What each service visit should cover

A proper service visit for a rental pool is not just a chemical check. It should include:

- Brushing the walls and floor, including the waterline
- Vacuuming or running the robotic cleaner and emptying its filter
- Emptying skimmer and pump baskets
- Checking and adjusting pH, alkalinity, and free chlorine
- Backwashing or rinsing the filter if pressure has risen
- Inspecting fittings, return jets, and the skimmer weir for damage

The waterline tile deserves particular attention. In Cyprus, the combination of hard water, high evaporation, and sunscreen creates a persistent scum line that looks bad to guests and is harder to remove the longer it's left. Wipe it at every visit during summer, not just occasionally.

## Managing Water Chemistry Between Visits

If you're not on site between visits and you can't have a professional call more than once a week, you need reliable slow-release sanitisation that can maintain free chlorine levels between services.

Trichlor tablets in a floating dispenser or an inline feeder are the most common approach, and they work. Just remember that they also add cyanuric acid over time, so monitor CYA levels across the season. An alternative is a salt chlorinator, which many villa pools in Cyprus now use. It reduces the chemical handling burden and can maintain more consistent free chlorine levels, but it still requires proper pH management and regular salt level checks. It is not a set-and-forget system.

After a dust storm — which can deposit fine particulates across the whole island — check the filter and water clarity before guests arrive, not after they complain about it.

## What Guests Actually Notice

Most guests aren't reading your chemical log. They're looking at whether the water is clear, whether the tiles are clean, whether the poolside is tidy, and whether anything looks broken or neglected.

Clear water with correct chemistry should be non-negotiable. Beyond that, pay attention to:

**Water colour.** Slightly blue-green, crystal clear. Any cloudiness, green tint, or visible algae is an immediate problem.

**Waterline and surrounds.** Scum, scale, and staining at the tile line are visually obvious and suggest neglect even when the water tests correctly.

**Equipment condition.** A cracked skimmer lid, a broken pool light, or a ladder with a loose fitting all create a poor impression and a potential liability.

**Poolside furniture and area.** Beyond the water itself, guests associate a dirty or cluttered pool deck with a poorly maintained pool.

## Before Every Check-In

Run through a short pre-arrival checklist: test the water the day before (or morning of), confirm filter pressure is normal, check the poolside is clean, verify the pump is running correctly, and make sure there's nothing floating in the pool that shouldn't be there. It takes twenty minutes and it eliminates most of the issues that generate complaints.
