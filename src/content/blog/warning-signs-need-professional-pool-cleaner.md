Owning a pool in Cyprus is a genuine pleasure for most of the year, but it also comes with real responsibilities. Most owners handle the basics themselves — skimming, adding chlorine, checking the pump — and that works well enough most of the time. The problem is knowing when the basics are no longer enough. Below are the clearest signs that your pool needs a professional, not another bag of shock treatment and a hopeful wait.

## The Water Has Gone Green, Cloudy, or Both

Green water means algae. Cloudy water usually means a chemical imbalance, a filtration problem, or both. Neither is unusual in Cyprus, where summer temperatures above 35°C and intense UV radiation accelerate algae growth and drive chlorine out of solution faster than it does in cooler climates.

### Why DIY treatment often falls short

When water is visibly green, most owners add a large dose of chlorine and run the pump overnight. Sometimes that works. Often it doesn't, because the underlying cause — low cyanuric acid levels, a poorly calibrated salt chlorinator, a biofilm layer coating the pipe walls — remains untouched. A professional will test for the full range of parameters: free chlorine, combined chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, cyanuric acid, calcium hardness, and phosphate levels. Treating the symptom without understanding the chemistry usually means the problem returns within a week or two.

Repeated algae outbreaks are a clear sign that something structural is wrong with your water chemistry or filtration setup, not just that you need more chemicals.

## Your Filter Is Running but the Water Isn't Clearing

A pump that runs and a pool that stays murky are a frustrating combination. In most cases, the filter is either overloaded, exhausted, or incorrectly sized for the pool volume.

Cyprus pools face a specific challenge here. The island's dust storms, which arrive most frequently in spring and early summer carrying fine Saharan particles, can overwhelm a sand or cartridge filter in a matter of days. That fine particulate doesn't always trigger a high pressure reading on the gauge; it can blind a filter without the usual warning signs. If you have backwashed the sand filter, cleaned the cartridge, and the water remains dull or hazy, the filter media itself may need replacing — or the pressure gauge may have failed and been masking the problem for months.

A professional can pull apart the filter housing, inspect the laterals or cartridge condition, assess whether the turnover rate is adequate for your pool size, and determine whether the equipment needs servicing or replacement.

## You Can See or Smell Signs of Scale or Corrosion

Cyprus has among the hardest tap water in Europe. The calcium carbonate content in mains water across Limassol, Paphos, and Larnaca is consistently high, and over time that calcium deposits on pool surfaces, tile grout lines, and inside the heater and pipework.

### Recognising the early warning signs

Scale often appears first as a white or grey tide line at the waterline, or as rough patches on plaster surfaces. Corroded copper fittings, greenish staining around return jets, or pitting on stainless steel ladder fittings all indicate that the water chemistry has drifted out of balance and has been doing so for some time. Left unchecked, scale inside a heat exchanger can reduce efficiency significantly and eventually cause the unit to fail.

Acid washing and descaling require proper equipment and handling. This is not a job for domestic products. A professional can also assess whether your calcium hardness and LSI (Langelier Saturation Index) are being managed correctly going forward, so the problem doesn't simply recur.

## The Pool Was Left for a Long Time Without Maintenance

This is common with holiday properties, seasonal rental villas, and pools belonging to owners who spend part of the year off the island. A pool left untreated for several weeks in a Cyprus summer is a different problem from one left for the same period in northern Europe. Heat, UV, and airborne debris combine quickly. What looked manageable before you left can become a black algae or mustard algae situation by the time you return, and those strains are significantly harder to eliminate than common green algae.

If you have returned to a pool that is dark green, has visible black or brown patches on the walls and floor, or has developed a strong sulphurous or musty smell, call a professional before adding anything. Black algae in particular has a protective outer layer that makes it resistant to standard chlorination; treating it incorrectly can push it deeper into plaster and make complete removal more difficult.

## Your Chemical Costs Keep Rising Without Improvement

If you are spending noticeably more on chemicals month over month and the pool still isn't performing well, the issue is almost certainly systemic. Common culprits include a leaking return fitting that is constantly diluting treated water, a salt cell that is under-producing because of scale buildup on the plates, or a pH that keeps drifting because total alkalinity has never been properly adjusted.

A professional visit in this situation usually pays for itself. Identifying and resolving the root cause is cheaper than continuing to pour chemicals into a pool that can't hold its balance.
